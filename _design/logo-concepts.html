Identity sheet · 3 concepts

A mark for FairShare — the split that math vouches for.

Three logo concepts, each rendered at the sizes a judge actually sees: 16 px in a browser tab, 28 px on a card, 64 px in the app header. Every mark is built from the same two-color vocabulary the app uses for its fairness verdict — violet = envy-free and amber = envy by one item — so the avatar and the product speak the same language.

Theme All stages render the dark variant by design — it is the brand ground.
01Concept
The Verdict Mark

Two circles, one item, one amber gap.

The mark is an EFX verdict. Two equal circles stand for two people; a small square is the contested item; a faint amber line shows the one-item gap that math allows when an item can't be cut. Violet dominates because that is the common case — most splits are envy-free. Amber appears only on the gap edge, the same way the app's results card uses amber only on the contested cell.

02Concept
The Honest Split

A single bar, divided, with one amber cell.

The mark is the result table from the results screen — one horizontal bar partitioned by the actual share, plus a small amber cell where envy survives. Reads as "this is the split we computed." The bar form survives any size: at 16 px it still reads as a divided line, at 64 px the amber cell becomes the focal point.

03Concept
The F Mark

An F that splits itself in two.

The boldest of the three: a custom-drawn letter F whose horizontal arms separate into two equal stacks — the top arm shows two violet bars (one wanted, one got), the bottom arm is a single amber bar (the one-item gap). It is the most "logo-shaped" of the three and works as a real wordmark. At 16 px the F + amber dot alone carry the brand.

My pick is 03 — the F Mark — because the judging is by a panel that will spend 3–5 seconds on the avatar before reading anything. A letter-mark with a clear amber accent reads as "this is a real product" instantly, and the amber cell next to the F quietly echoes the same amber cell that appears on every EFX result. 01 is the most semantically pure; 02 is the most literal; 03 is the one I'd bet on if I were the entry.
